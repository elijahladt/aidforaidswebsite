Public Health Protocols

The 4 Essential Pillars of Disease Prevention

Infectious pathogens spread through contact, droplets, air, and fluids. Here is how modern biomedical science and public health stop transmission in its tracks.

HIV, Hep B/C, STIs

Sexual & Bloodborne

Clinical safeguards including daily or injectable PrEP (~99% effective), emergency PEP within 72 hours, latex barriers, and harm reduction.

  • U=U Consensus: Undetectable = Untransmittable.
  • Routine Testing: At least every 3–6 months.
  • Harm Reduction: Never share needles.
Read Detailed Protocols →
Flu, COVID, RSV

Airborne & Droplets

Managing indoor aerosol density through ventilation, HEPA filtration, high-filtration masks (N95), and annual seasonal immunization.

  • Filtration: Wear N95/KN95 in surges.
  • Fresh Air: Open windows, MERV-13 filters.
  • Isolation: Stay home when symptomatic.
Read Detailed Protocols →
Norovirus, MRSA

Hand & Surface Hygiene

Interrupting fomite transfer by washing hands with soap and water for 20 seconds, using 60%+ alcohol sanitizers, and disinfecting high-touch objects.

  • 20 Seconds: Scrub palms, fingers, nails.
  • T-Zone Rule: Avoid touching eyes, nose, mouth.
  • Disinfection: EPA-registered wipes for phones.
Read Detailed Protocols →
Salmonella, Hep A

Food & Water Safety

Adhering to the CDC's 4 core safety steps: Clean, Separate, Cook to safe internal temperatures, and Chill promptly to prevent enteric outbreaks.

  • Cook Temps: 165°F poultry, 160°F ground meats.
  • Separate: Keep raw meats isolated.
  • Chilling: Refrigerate under 40°F within 2h.

Aid for AIDS of Nevada (AFAN):
Las Vegas's Frontline Lifeline

Founded in 1984 in Las Vegas, Aid for AIDS of Nevada (AFAN) is the oldest and largest AIDS service organization in the Silver State. AFAN provides comprehensive support and advocacy for adults and children living with and affected by HIV/AIDS in Southern Nevada.
