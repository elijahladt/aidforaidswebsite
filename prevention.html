The Complete Disease Prevention Guide

Disease prevention is built upon interrupting transmission pathways. By combining modern clinical interventions (like PrEP and ART) with behavioral hygiene and community harm reduction, we can halt infectious disease transmission across Las Vegas and Southern Nevada.

Pillar 1 • High Priority

Sexual & Bloodborne Pathogen Prevention

HIV, Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, Syphilis, Gonorrhea, Chlamydia

PrEP (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis)

PrEP is a highly safe, FDA-approved biomedical prevention method. Taken as a daily pill (Truvada or Descovy) or received as a long-acting injection every 2 months (Apretude), PrEP reduces sexual transmission of HIV by approximately 99% and injection drug use transmission by at least 74%.

• Need PrEP in Las Vegas? Aid for AIDS of Nevada (AFAN) coordinates navigation and co-pay relief!
PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis) — 72h Window

If you have had a possible exposure to HIV (unprotected sex, condom failure, or shared needle), you must start PEP strictly within 72 hours. PEP consists of antiretroviral medications taken for 28 days that stop HIV from reproducing and establishing infection.

• Head to an emergency room or call AFAN ((702) 382-2326) immediately for prompt linkage.
U=U (Undetectable = Untransmittable)

Supported by the CDC, WHO, and NIH: A person living with HIV who takes antiretroviral therapy (ART) as prescribed and maintains an undetectable viral load (<200 copies/mL) cannot transmit HIV to sexual partners. Adherence to treatment is prevention!

Barrier Protections & Safer Sex

  • ✓ External & Internal Condoms: Highly effective physical barrier blocking fluids and reducing STI transmission.
  • ✓ Water-based Lubricants: Prevent microscopic friction tears in mucous membranes during intimacy.
  • ✓ Routine STI Panels: Regular screening every 3 to 6 months catches asymptomatic chlamydia, syphilis, and gonorrhea.

Harm Reduction & Syringe Access in Clark County

Sharing injection equipment or preparation items transmits bloodborne pathogens rapidly. Southern Nevada operates legal harm reduction exchanges:

  • • Trac-B Exchange: 6114 W. Charleston Blvd, Las Vegas. Free sterile supplies.
  • • Syringe Vending Machines: Discreet, 24/7 access to clean kits and safe disposal boxes.
  • • Naloxone (Narcan): Always keep emergency overdose reversal kits on hand.

Pillar 2

Airborne & Respiratory Viral Protection

Influenza (Flu), SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19), RSV, Tuberculosis

1. High-Efficiency Masks

Cloth masks offer limited aerosol protection. Use certified N95, KN95, or KF94 respirators that filter ≥95% of airborne aerosols, especially during winter viral surges, transit, and healthcare clinics.

Source: CDC & NIOSH

2. Indoor Air & Ventilation

Viral particles linger like smoke in closed rooms. Utilize HEPA air purifiers, maintain MERV-13 air filtration in HVAC systems, and maximize outdoor air exchange to reduce aerosol concentration.

Source: ASHRAE Standards

3. Seasonal Immunization

Annual influenza shots and updated COVID-19 boosters drastically lower viral loads, hospitalization, and transmission rates to high-risk immunocompromised neighbors.

Source: SNHD Immunization
Pillar 3

Surface, Hand & Fomite Hygiene

Norovirus, MRSA, Clostridioides difficile, Enteroviruses

Contact transmission happens when infectious bodily fluids or bacteria transfer to hands, touch surfaces, and enter mucosal membranes (eyes, nose, mouth).

The 5 Steps to Effective Handwashing:

  1. Wet hands with clean, running water (warm or cold).
  2. Apply soap and lather thoroughly.
  3. Scrub for at least 20 seconds: palms, backs, between fingers, and under nails.
  4. Rinse well under clean running water.
  5. Dry using a clean towel or air dry.

When Soap & Water Are Unavailable:

Use an alcohol-based hand sanitizer containing at least 60% ethanol or isopropanol. Apply to all surfaces and rub vigorously until hands feel completely dry (~20 seconds).

Note: Hand sanitizer does NOT kill Norovirus or C. diff. Always prefer soap and friction handwashing after using restrooms or caring for someone with stomach illness!

Pillar 4

Food & Waterborne Pathogen Control

Salmonella, Campylobacter, E. coli, Hepatitis A

Step 1

Clean

Wash hands for 20 seconds, sanitize cutting boards, knives, and counters before and after raw poultry and meats.

Step 2

Separate

Never place cooked food on an unwashed plate that held raw meat. Keep raw juices segregated in the refrigerator.

Step 3

Cook

Cook poultry to 165°F, ground beef to 160°F, and pork/fish to 145°F. Measure using an internal food thermometer.

Step 4

Chill

Refrigerate perishables below 40°F within 2 hours. In hot Nevada summer heat (>90°F), refrigerate within 1 hour!
